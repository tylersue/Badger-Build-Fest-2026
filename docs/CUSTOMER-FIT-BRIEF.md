# Customer Fit Brief

The short version of [CUSTOMER-FIT.md](CUSTOMER-FIT.md). Every number here is in the full document with its source and verification status; anything marked [unverified] or [assumed] carries that label there too. Date: 2026-09-26.

**The question:** who do we say we are for, at which zoom level, to which audience, and which one person goes on screen for sixty seconds.

## The answer in five lines

1. **On screen:** a UW senior whose AI-polished applications got zero callbacks, uploading her resume to a career coach's or recruiter's agent. Scores 93/100 and ranks first under every weighting tried.
2. **Where next:** the pet owner holding a vet estimate (83/100, and a Cost-of-Care challenge hook), the student-loan borrower whose rules changed three times in 2026, the vibe coder with an exposed database, then tax in January.
3. **Break Card:** the student founder who uploads a pitch deck, asks what valuation to put on a SAFE, and gets a refusal plus the founder's calendar link.
4. **Opening quote:** the September 4, 2026 r/UWMadison post. A SuccessWorks-approved resume, 0 for 12 on campus jobs, and a hiring administrator replying "100+ applications regardless of the role."
5. **The gate:** which expert says yes by Saturday night. If a vet tech does and no coach does, the pet owner moves to first.

---

## 1. Who fits

**Hirer pattern A, "AI-native and burned."** A growing population that already does most of its work with generic AI, hits a question where the AI is confidently wrong or refuses, and finds the human who knows costs $100 to $350 an hour. They do not need to be sold on chat. They need a named human behind the answer.

**Hirer pattern B, "a document and a deadline."** Someone holds a resume, an essay, a lease, a W-2, an estimate, or a deck, and something happens on a date.

**Expert pattern, "the unpaid help desk."** The tax guy, the PT friend, the coach everyone texts. Tacit, repetitive, free knowledge; no audience, so Delphi and Personify do not want them.

**The nine-line fit test.** A pair is a demo candidate at seven or more: holds a document; has a date; no license governs the advice; the expert's knowledge is tacit and repetitive; the refusal is demoable and not scary; an expert can be interviewed on camera within two weeks; receipts are collectable on campus this weekend; the population is visibly growing; a general judge recognizes the problem in one sentence.

## 2. The zoom ladder

Lead with the specific person. Widen one level per sentence. Never lead at level 4; never end at level 1.

| Level | Hirer | Expert | The number to say |
|---|---|---|---|

| **L3 Broad** | The advice-seeker priced out of professional rates who needs one accountable answer | Independent advice professionals with no audience and no content | About 26M with one paid question a year [assumed]; about 934,000 independent experts [assumed]; Delphi serves the 2,000 with audiences |
| **L2 Basic** | A category with a season and a document: applicants, filers, patients, renters, founders, pet owners, new hires | Low-liability categories: IECs, career coaches, recruiters, enrolled agents, trainers, vet techs, home-trades educators | 1.53M students filed 10.8M Common App applications with a counselor at 372:1; 886,338 PTIN holders; 20,000+ IECs |
| **L1 Specific** | One named person at one moment: the senior, 0 for 12, resume in hand | One practitioner we can interview this week: an alum recruiter, a former admissions reader, a VITA volunteer, a Rec Well trainer | n=1 on screen, n=11 in the receipts |

**Market size, with honesty labels.** Supply ceiling about $2.2B GMV a year [assumed]; demand check about $125M [assumed]; central case about $0.5B; SAM about $56M across roughly 23,000 experts in legally serviceable categories [assumed]; SOM about $1.8M at 1,000 experts in three years [assumed]. Say plainly that supply is abundant and demand is what we must prove. Proof that people pay for expert help inside software: TurboTax Live is 53% of a $5.3B business and grew 37%; JustAnswer runs 8,500 to 9,500 paid questions a day.

**Which level for which audience.** Applied AI judges: L1 person plus the working loop. DoIT (Badgers for Badgers): L1 campus person, the friction point, the receipts count, nothing off campus. Strato VC (Open Venture): L1 person, then L3 market with the demand caveat, then the 85/15 model. Art of the Break: the L1 scenario as the attack surface and a measured failure rate.

## 4. The ranking

**What judges score.** The official BuildFest 2026 criteria, verbatim from Devpost: Technical Execution, Innovation & Originality, Impact & Feasibility ("Does this project address a real-world problem effectively?"), and Presentation & Demo ("How clearly did the team communicate their problem statement, solution, and value proposition?"). Two of the four are decided by which user and moment go on screen. Last year's organizer rewarded "problem definition, market insight, and venture readiness," and all three 2025 podium projects had a student as the user.

**The rubric.** Ten dimensions, weights summing to 100: problem recognizability 15, single user and moment 10, sixty-second demo-ability 15, campus receipts this weekend 12, expert reachability this weekend 10 (also a gate), founder-market fit 8, liability cleanliness 8, audience size 8, why-now strength 7, challenge fit 7.

**Corrections that change the script.**

- No UW career fair is within 48 hours. Sources disagree on the fall dates; only October 7 appears in two independent fetches. Use the October 7 fair or the September 4 Reddit moment.
- The job-seeker pain is an outcome problem, not a queue problem. The campus report found no wait-time complaints. Say "followed the free advice and was rejected twelve times."
- The market is crowded (LinkedIn, Teal, Jobscan, Handshake AI). One sentence of differentiation is mandatory: a named human, their own words with citations, a refusal with a link.
- Devpost's rules say "Projects must be started during the event (no pre-written project code)." Phases 1 to 3 are on `main`. Ask an organizer before submitting.

## 5. What to say

**The problem sentence, one per top candidate.**

- *Job seeker:* Ten days after the career fair, a UW senior with fourteen recruiter conversations and zero follow-ups pastes her resume into a recruiter's agent and reads, in the recruiter's own words, why "assisted with" on three bullets reads as "did nothing."
- *Pet owner:* The night before a $1,940 dental, a student uploads the clinic estimate to a veterinary practice manager's agent and learns, in her words, which three lines are pre-anesthetic screening to keep and which one is reasonable to defer.
- *Applicant:* Six weeks before the December 1 deadline, an applicant uploads her statement to a former admissions reader's agent and hears, in the reader's words, that her opening is the same "ever since I was a child" opener he skipped four hundred times a season.

**The bridge.** "She is one of the 1.5 million students who will send 23 applications per posting this year, most written by the same three AI tools, to recruiters who spend half their week filtering them. Behind her are 128 million Americans who already ask an AI before they ask a professional. On the other side is the recruiter who gets five 'can you look at my resume' messages a week and answers none of them for money."

**Where next (1:50 to 2:00).** Career and admissions on this campus in October, because nothing is licensed. Cost-of-care with a vet tech's agent, because 52% of pet owners skipped care over cost. Tax in January with enrolled agents. Student-loan borrowers whose rules changed three times this year. Vibe coders uploading a schema to a senior engineer's agent. Every unpaid help desk after that.

**Why nobody solved it.** OpenAI barred tailored licensed advice in October 2025 and is retiring custom GPTs in December. Delphi prices its tiers by audience size and only serves experts who bring followers. And the clone critics' own complaint (Dewey, May 2026) is that clones cannot say "I don't know" or hand off to the human, which is the only thing ours is built to do.

"Students loved it." Any TAM without a label.

## 6. The six forcing questions, answered for the job seeker

1. **Demand reality.** None yet; nobody has used it. Proxies: the Sep 4 post with 60 upvotes, JustAnswer's daily paid volume, Personify's first coach taking $8,800 in 24 hours (vendor-reported). The receipts plan is the first real answer.
2. **Status quo.** SuccessWorks 10 to 3, L&S only, about 22 professional advisers; Engineering has four. After 3 pm: ChatGPT, Reddit, a friend. The cost is outcomes, not queues.
3. **Desperate specificity.** The L&S junior in the Sep 4 post: cover letter for every job, resume blessed by the office, denied twelve times, in a 100-applicant pile. Find her equivalent in the building Saturday.
4. **Narrowest wedge.** One coach's agent, one resume upload, three cited fixes, one refusal that books the human, for cents. That is the current build. The marketplace is the "where next."
5. **Observation and surprise.** Not yet. The weakest answer and the one DoIT scores. The receipts questions are designed to produce a surprise, not a compliment.
6. **Future-fit.** When both sides of hiring are machines, the scarce input is a named human's judgment about what reads as real. The risk: LinkedIn or Handshake hires the coaches. The answer: neither pays the expert 85% of anything, and neither refuses.

## 7. Receipts and open questions

**The sentence the judges should hear.** "We approached N students; M talked to us; K had one of these problems in the last 30 days; J tried it; and the thing we thought mattered, X, was the thing they cared least about."

**Where and when.** BuildFest floor at Morgridge Hall, Saturday 1 to 3 pm (open until 7, away game). Union South, Saturday 3 to 4 pm and 7 to 8 pm, and Sunday 8 to 10:15 am, the only busy indoor spot before the deadline. College Library first floor, Saturday 5 to 6:30 pm, for the essay crowd. Two people per location: one talks, one logs. No table, no UW marks, no selling, no chalk, no photos of anyone's document.

**Five questions in four minutes.** Did you need advice on a resume, essay, taxes, lease, visa form, or training plan in the last 30 days? Who did you ask and how long until you trusted the answer? Would you upload that document to get a named coach's feedback with the exact line cited back? After a 90-second demo: what did you expect it to do that it did not, and which part did you not care about? If it refused and offered to book the human, plus or minus? Report the denominator first, the miss second, the surprise third, two verbatim quotes with consent, and what you did not do.
