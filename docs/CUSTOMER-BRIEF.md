## The story in one line

**Ask a real expert's AI, built from an interview with them, that cites only their words and refuses the rest, for cents a message.**

## Who we're NOT for

Experts who already have an audience (that's Delphi's market). Anyone who wants the agent to diagnose, prescribe, pick investments, or fill in a tax return. Therapists, financial advisors, immigration paralegals, and vets in year one: AI-therapy bans, Advisers Act rules, unauthorized-practice rules, and vet-client rules make each a liability story.

## Evidence the market exists

- **People already pay strangers for expert answers.** JustAnswer: 12,000 experts, 700 categories, $28–$125/month. The FTC sued them in January 2026 for the subscription trap. Demand is real; the incumbent's trust is broken.
- **People already pay for expert help inside software.** TurboTax Live is 53% of a $5.3B business and grew 37% last year.
- **Experts will let an AI answer for them.** Delphi: $16M Series A led by Sequoia, 2,000+ experts, one creator at seven figures on $39/month. Personify launched April 2026. Both require an existing audience. We don't.
- **Generic AI is failing on exactly these questions.** 57% wrong on standard financial questions, 88% on hard ones (18 models, 10,000+ answers). Half of chatbot health answers were problematic (BMJ Open, April 2026). r/tax threads document ChatGPT using last year's standard deduction.
- **Half of US adults now use chatbots,** and a fifth ask them for medical advice (Pew, 2026). Only 18% rate the health answers highly accurate vs 65% for their own provider.

## Why now

- **Oct 29, 2025:** OpenAI's policy bars tailored licensed advice without a licensed professional involved. A named, consenting expert is the compliant shape.
- **Dec 11, 2026:** custom GPTs stop running. The GPT Store never broadly paid creators. The biggest consumer "build an agent" surface is leaving.
- **Tokens are cheap:** a typical grounded message costs about 1.5 cents on Sonnet 5.
- **Law is moving toward verified self-clones:** NO FAKES Act, FTC impersonation rules, Illinois's AI-therapy ban (Aug 2025), New York and Pennsylvania bills against bots posing as licensed professionals.

## Money

- **Cost per message:** about 1.5 cents typical, 5 cents heavy.
- **Price at the default 3x multiplier:** 4.56 cents. Expert earns 2.58, platform 0.46 plus cost recovery. A 10-message session is 46 cents vs $28 minimum at JustAnswer.
- **Honest sizing:** ~934k independent advice professionals in the US → ~$2.2B GMV supply ceiling. Demand is the real limit: ~$125M GMV at plausible conversion. Say **$0.5B central case** and that demand is what we must prove.
- **At MVP volume an expert earns tens of dollars, not hundreds.** The MVP proves the metering loop and the 85/15 split, not an income.

## The pitch in 60 seconds

1. **Problem:** AI gets financial questions wrong 57% of the time, and 57% of people who ask would act on the answer anyway.
2. **Why now:** OpenAI just exited tailored advice and is retiring GPTs; tokens cost cents; the law wants verified self-clones.
3. **Wedge:** college and career advice on this campus, this month. Tax in January.
4. **Demo:** interview a coach → student asks a question, gets her cited answer → uploads an essay, gets rubric feedback → asks admission odds, gets a refusal and a link to book her → coach's dashboard shows credits earned.
5. **Model:** cents per message, expert keeps 85% of the markup. We only make money when the expert does.
6. **Ask:** the prize, ten intros to counselors and enrolled agents, a small pre-seed for a tax-season pilot.

**Strongest objection, "why doesn't OpenAI do this?":** they just banned tailored licensed advice and are killing the product that would host it. Their creator push copies Patreon, which needs an audience. Ours doesn't.
