Who we're for

Expert (supply)

Who
A practicing advisor with no audience and no tech skill: independent college consultant, former admissions officer, campus career coach. From January: solo tax preparer (EA, AFSP, one-partner CPA).
Trigger
The fifth "can you look at my essay" this week. Tax pros: April 20 or October 16, burnout fresh, off-season open.
Uses today
Free intro calls, a course nobody buys, the same checklist emailed over and over.

Answer the same 30 questions once. Earn from people who can't afford a package. Zero liability in admissions.

Hirer (demand)

Who
A student or applicant with a document and a deadline: resume, personal statement, essay, later a W-2. Priced out of $140–$349 an hour.
Trigger
A career fair in 48 hours, a Nov 1 deadline, a grad application due.
Uses today
ChatGPT ("generic AI slop"), Reddit, a school counselor with 372 other students.
Why switch
A named expert's rubric applied to their own upload, cited, at 11 pm, for cents.
Must see in session 1
Upload a draft, get feedback tied to the expert's framework, see a refusal that offers the human.

Not for: experts who already have an audience (Delphi's market); anyone wanting diagnosis, prescriptions, investment picks, or a filled-in return; therapists, financial advisors, immigration paralegals, and vets in year one (AI-therapy bans, Advisers Act, unauthorized-practice and vet-client rules).

Evidence the market exists

  • People already pay strangers for expert answers. JustAnswer: 12,000 experts, 700 categories, $28–$125/month. The FTC sued them in January 2026 over the subscription trap. Demand is real; the incumbent's trust is broken.
  • People pay for expert help inside software. TurboTax Live is 53% of a $5.3B business and grew 37% last year.
  • Experts will let an AI answer for them. Delphi: $16M Series A led by Sequoia, 2,000+ experts, one creator at seven figures on $39/month. Personify launched April 2026. Both require an audience. We don't.
  • Generic AI fails on exactly these questions. 57% wrong on standard financial questions, 88% on hard ones (18 models, 10,000+ answers). Half of chatbot health answers problematic (BMJ Open, April 2026). r/tax threads document ChatGPT using last year's standard deduction.
  • Half of US adults use chatbots, a fifth for medical advice (Pew, 2026). Only 18% rate the health answers highly accurate vs 65% for their own provider.

Why now

  • Oct 29, 2025: OpenAI bars tailored licensed advice without a licensed professional involved. A named, consenting expert is the compliant shape.
  • Dec 11, 2026: custom GPTs stop running; the GPT Store never broadly paid creators. The biggest consumer "build an agent" surface is leaving.
  • Tokens are cheap: a typical grounded message costs about 1.5¢ on Sonnet 5.
  • Law is moving toward verified self-clones: NO FAKES Act, FTC impersonation rules, Illinois's AI-therapy ban (Aug 2025), New York and Pennsylvania bills against bots posing as licensed professionals.

Money

  • Cost per message: about 1.5¢ typical, 5¢ heavy.
  • Price at 3×: 4.56¢. Expert earns 2.58¢, platform 0.46¢ plus cost recovery. A 10-message session is 46¢ vs a $28 minimum month at JustAnswer.
  • Honest sizing: ~934k independent US advice professionals → ~$2.2B GMV supply ceiling. Demand is the real limit: ~$125M GMV at plausible conversion. Say $0.5B central case and that demand is what we must prove.
  • At MVP volume an expert earns tens of dollars, not hundreds. The MVP proves the metering loop and the 85/15 split, not an income.

The pitch in 60 seconds

  1. Problem: AI gets financial questions wrong 57% of the time, and 57% of people who ask would act on the answer anyway.
  2. Why now: OpenAI just exited tailored advice and is retiring GPTs; tokens cost cents; the law wants verified self-clones.
  3. Wedge: college and career advice on this campus, this month. Tax in January.
  4. Demo: interview a coach → a student asks and gets her cited answer → uploads an essay, gets rubric feedback → asks admission odds, gets a refusal and a link to book her → the coach's dashboard shows credits earned.
  5. Model: cents per message; the expert keeps 85% of the markup. We only make money when the expert does.
  6. Ask: the prize, ten intros to counselors and enrolled agents, a small pre-seed for a tax-season pilot.

Strongest objection, "why doesn't OpenAI do this?": they just banned tailored licensed advice and are killing the product that would host it. Their creator push copies Patreon, which needs an audience. Ours doesn't.
