### Why nobody has solved it
The people who know what a specific school's readers reward (former admissions readers, school counselors, IECs) sell that judgment by the hour and cannot scale past roughly 2,800 IECA members and 19% of a public counselor's week, while every free or AI tool produces the polished-generic voice that half of admissions officers already view unfavorably and that Caltech and Yale now say can get an admission rescinded, so the market has plenty of drafting help and almost no scalable, attributable reading help.

### Fit
Interviewed experts: former admissions readers, high-school college counselors, and independent educational consultants (and for the grad-school extension, former law- and medical-school admissions officers); the hirer, a first-generation applicant or a UW-Madison senior applying to law or medical school, uploads a Common App personal statement or grad-school statement of purpose and asks "does this sound like me, and what would a reader at this school flag," gets answers grounded only in that reader's own interview with citations, and gets a refusal plus a link to the human for anything the reader did not cover (a specific school's supplement, financial-aid appeals, disciplinary disclosures).

### Why nobody has solved it
The people who actually know the answers are either forbidden to give them (ISS: "does not advise on taxes or tax law"; 8 CFR 292.1 for paid immigration advice), priced for forms rather than questions (Sprintax at $54.95), or too scarce to scale (one ISS office for ~7,000 F-1 students during a year with six federal rule changes), while general chatbots now either refuse tailored tax/immigration advice or answer with last year's rules — so the student is left with FAQ pages and the sentence "each student's situation is different."

### Fit
Interview a nonresident-tax specialist (enrolled agent or CPA who files 1040-NR/8843 for F/J students, ideally one who works with Wisconsin returns) and, for the immigration-adjacent questions, a licensed immigration attorney who advises on F-1/CPT/OPT/H-1B; the hirer uploads their I-20 or DS-2019, I-94, W-2/1042-S, scholarship letter or last year's 1040-NR and asks "do I owe Wisconsin tax on my fellowship?" or "is my summer internship still CPT-eligible under the Aug 25 rule?", getting cited answers in the expert's own words or a refusal plus a booking link to that human.

### Counter-evidence / risk
- **Free beats cents-per-message for this cohort:** Google gives eligible U.S. college students 18+ a "Free student plan for 1 year" of Google AI Pro (redeem until Dec 31 2026, then "$19.99/month" unless cancelled): https://gemini.google/students/ — [primary] [self-reported]; ChatGPT's free tier already serves the 18–25 group that sends ~46% of messages (NBER w34255). A student who can get a 700M-user chatbot for $0 must be convinced that a named human's grounded answer is worth paying for.
- **They like the free advice:** Wells Fargo found that of those who acted on AI money suggestions, "nearly all (90%) say those ideas were profitable or worthwhile" (https://newsroom.wf.com/news-releases/news-details/2026/Wells-Fargo-2026-Money-Study-Reveals-Americans-Redefining-the-American-Dream-Gen-Z-leaning-on-Parents-for-Financial-Support/default.aspx — [self-reported survey]); Inside Higher Ed students rate AI's effect on their learning positive (27%) far more often than negative (7%) (https://www.insidehighered.com/news/students/academics/2025/08/29/survey-college-students-views-ai). Satisfaction, not accuracy, is what the incumbent optimizes — and it is high.
- **Paying is still the exception, and the numbers conflict:** PNC card data (Aug 2026): "about 2% of US households pay for a generative AI subscription" (https://theslowai.substack.com/p/only-2-percent-pay-for-ai — [secondary, citing CBS/PNC]); Menlo Ventures' July 2026 survey instead reports "55% of AI users now pay for at least one AI product" (https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/ — [self-reported survey]). Either way the paying base is small or newly formed, so cents-per-message has to be sold as accountability (a named human, citations, a refusal), not access.

### Why nobody has solved it
General chatbots are the default first stop because they are free, instant and satisfying, but the moment they give tailored licensed advice they are now policy-barred (OpenAI, Oct 29 2025), regulator-flagged (FCA: "not regulated"), and litigated (JCCP 5431; Pennsylvania v. Character.ai; the pulmonary-embolism suit) — while real advisers (campus career, admissions, financial aid, tax) cannot answer thousands of cents-sized questions — so nobody offers the middle product: a named expert's own words, cited, with a refusal and a human hand-off when the question leaves what they know.

### Fit
Interview the people students already queue for — career advisers, admissions counselors, financial-aid officers, licensed therapists (for the boundary cases) and a nonresident-tax CPA — and publish each as an agent; the hirer uploads a résumé, offer letter, transcript, financial-aid award letter, lease or tax form, asks "should I take this offer / does this essay work / can I afford this," and gets answers grounded only in that expert's interview with citations, or a refusal plus a link to book the human.

### Why nobody has solved it

### Fit

### Why nobody has solved it

### Counter-evidence / risk
- **AI is already deflating the low end of paid expertise.** Fiverr's Q2 2026 revenue fell 10% to $97.8M and active buyers fell 21.9% to 2.7M; CEO Micha Kaufman: "While AI absorbs high-volume, low-value, transactional tasks, it is also unlocking the need for longer duration projects where AI tools enhance human expertise"; FY2026 guidance is a 14-17% revenue decline. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1762301/000117891326003624/exhibit_99-1.htm and https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/29/3334963/0/en/fiverr-announces-second-quarter-2026-results.html (Jul 29, 2026) — [primary]. Read together: the trust gap is the wedge (people want a named human behind the answer), but price expectations are being set at zero.

### Why nobody has solved it
The binding constraint is the expert's hours, and every fix so far either adds more humans (43 states rewriting CPA licensure, career-center budgets up 21%) or removes the human entirely (free chatbots people do not trust); nobody has made one practitioner's judgment reusable at cents per answer while keeping that practitioner accountable and reachable.

### Fit
The product converts the scarce, inflating hour into a reusable, cited asset: a student or taxpayer gets the expert's own words for cents, the refusal-plus-handoff routes the hard cases back to the human whose rate keeps rising, and the 85% share lets the same practitioner earn from questions they no longer have hours to take.

### Counter-evidence / risk
- **Thin-demand precedents.** Google Answers (2002-2006) shut down because "the Answers community's limited size and other product considerations" made it not worth continuing ( https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Answers ); Clarity.fm has not reported growth since its 2015 acquisition and its site now renders as an empty shell; Wisdom's founder said users came for "personal development, wellness, mindfulness, and mental health" rather than the mentorship marketplace she built, forcing a pivot and sale to Noom ( https://www.uktech.news/funding/vc-funding/wisdom-noom-vibe-20240828 , Aug 28, 2024).
- **JustAnswer's growth is entangled with dark patterns, and the low-end gig market is shrinking.** The FTC alleges JustAnswer enrolled "hundreds of thousands" into unwanted subscriptions ( https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/JustAnswerComplaint_FILED.pdf ), so its traffic growth is not clean evidence of willingness to pay per answer; meanwhile Fiverr's active buyers fell 21.9% in a year as "AI absorbs high-volume, low-value, transactional tasks" ( https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/29/3334963/0/en/fiverr-announces-second-quarter-2026-results.html ), which is exactly the tier a cents-per-message product sits in.

### Why nobody has solved it
Every incumbent sells the expert's time (per-minute calls, monthly subscriptions, booked sessions, gigs), so unit economics push them to high prices ($28+/month, $450/session, $15,000/call) or to subscription tricks, and the AI entrants that are cheap drop the accountable human; none sells one named expert's knowledge asynchronously, with citations to their own words and a refusal boundary that hands off to them.

### Fit
Paid demand for expert answers is proven at scale (27M JustAnswer questions, $4B of Upwork GSV, 50% growth in AI-consulting GSV, $15,000 calls on Intro) but is priced in hours and subscriptions that a student or first-time taxpayer will not buy; cents per message with an 85% expert share and a human handoff captures that unserved tail, while the FTC action makes transparent per-message pricing a differentiator rather than a detail.

| MentorCruise | "1-on-1 Mentorship in Career" | mentees; senior tech mentors | Mentee pays; mentor keeps 80% | N ("merely the way to connect the two sides") |
| Minnect | "Human Answers for Human Decisions" | people needing "business guidance, health advice, or career direction" | Asker pays per response; expert keeps 80% | N |
| Circle AI Agents | agents "trained on your community" | community builders, course creators, coaches | Creator pays Circle Plus | Y |
| Kajabi Expert Agents | "AI teammates trained on your content" | "real experts whose knowledge is earned through lived experience" | Creator pays $69–$399 | Y |

Takeaway for the pitch: every clone platform (Delphi, Personify, Coachvox, BuddyPro, Steno, Circle, Kajabi) prices the expert as the customer and assumes the expert distributes; the platforms that supply demand (JustAnswer, MentorCruise, Minnect, Intro) are human-time marketplaces with 20–80% expert cuts and no grounded-AI layer. Delphi's Mar 2026 Discover hub is the first sign a clone platform is moving toward supplying demand, but only for already-famous minds.
