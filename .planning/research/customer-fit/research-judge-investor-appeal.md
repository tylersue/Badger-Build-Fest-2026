# What wins judges and seed investors: choosing the on-screen ICP for the Badger BuildFest 2026 demo video

Research date: 2026-09-26. Scope: judging rubrics (hackathon + university pitch competitions + this event), seed-investor marketplace frameworks, 2024-2026 winning student pitches, founder-market fit for student teams, recognizability statistics for the eight candidate scenarios, a weighted scoring rubric, scores, and problem statements for the top three.

Source rules used: every web claim carries a URL and a year. Text inside quotation marks was retrieved verbatim from the page (or from a PDF I extracted locally). Anything labeled **[snippet]** came from a search-engine summary of a page I could not open this session; treat it as directionally right but re-verify before putting it on a slide. **[unverified]** means I could not find a public source.

---

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Put the job seeker on screen (candidate 1).** It scores first under every weighting I tried (93/100 base). It is the only scenario where every judge in the room has personally had the problem, the document (a resume) is the most demo-safe upload possible, receipts are trivially collectable in Morgridge Hall, the "why now" (AI-polished applications flooding recruiters) is the best-evidenced of the eight, and it fits all three challenges the team cares about.
2. **Runner-up is the pet owner with a vet estimate (candidate 6, 83/100).** It has the cleanest refusal in the set (diagnosis needs an exam, a legal line, not just a knowledge gap), the largest implied market, and the 2026 Devpost page lists a challenge literally named "Cost-of-Care Conversation." Its weaknesses are receipts and expert reach this weekend.
3. **Use the student founder / SAFE refusal (candidate 2) as the Break Card scene, not the ICP.** It is the best Art-of-the-Break moment (5/5) but the narrowest market (2/5) and the least legible to non-VC judges.
4. **One factual correction for candidate 1:** there is no UW career fair "in 48 hours." The All-Campus Career & Internship Fair page lists September 16, 2026 at the Kohl Center; the next listed fair is the Inclusive Community Career & Internship Fair on October 7, 2026. Reframe the moment as "the fair was ten days ago, fourteen recruiter conversations, zero follow-ups" or "next fair is eleven days out." Details in section 5.

Rubric weights (base): recognizability 15, single-moment concreteness 10, 60-second demo-ability 15, campus receipts feasibility 12, expert reachability this weekend 10, founder-market fit 8, liability/regulatory cleanliness 8, implied audience size 8, investor why-now 7, challenge fit 7.

Ranked (base weights, out of 100): 1 job seeker 93.0; 6 pet owner 83.0; 3 applicant 78.0; 2 student founder 77.4; 5 renter 77.0; 4 international student 62.4; 7 rehab patient 54.4; 8 non-student founder 53.6.

### 1f. Recurring criteria, consolidated

1. A real, specific problem the judge can recognize ("genuine problem", "real need", "real friction", "problem behind the solution").
2. A working demo that shows the problem being solved, not features ("Does the hack work?", "Presentation & Demo", "the demo video gives the most amount of scope").
3. Evidence: users, validation, traction, even tiny ("tested assumptions", "100 accounts", "validated with fellow students").
4. Differentiation and originality ("not just connecting APIs together", "sustainable competitive differentiation").
5. Path beyond the weekend ("venture readiness", "credible venture pathways", "potential to grow into a viable product, venture, or community tool").
6. Team credibility and coachability ("Build what you know, and for who you know", "Are they coachable?").

## 2. What seed investors ask about two-sided and expert marketplaces

### 2a. Frameworks

- **Bill Gurley, "All Markets Are Not Created Equal," Nov 13, 2012.** Ten factors: New Experience vs. the Status Quo; Economic Advantages vs. the Status Quo; Opportunity for Technology to Add Value; High Fragmentation ("High buyer and supplier fragmentation is a huge positive for an online marketplace."); Friction of Supplier Sign-Up; Size of the Market Opportunity ("A proper TAM (total available market) analysis is imperative, but it is easy to make mistakes."); Expand the Market; Frequency ("A higher frequency is obviously better"); Payment Flow ("Being part of the payment flow is superior to not being a part of the payment flow."); Network Effects ("Network effects are rare but golden. If you don't have one try to find one."). https://abovethecrowd.com/2012/11/13/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces/ (2012)
- **a16z Marketplace Glossary.** Liquidity: "The ease with which buyers and sellers can find the right counterpart in the marketplace." Take rate: "The percentage of the gross merchandise value (GMV) captured by the marketplace." Managed marketplace: "Marketplaces that take on additional activities in order to better establish trust, especially in high-value or high-stakes categories." Leakage: "When a platform's supply-side and demand-side users utilize the marketplace for discovery, but then complete the transaction outside of the platform." Which side first: "It's typically easier to jumpstart supply than demand because suppliers are economically motivated. The harder part of scaling a marketplace is figuring out how to aggregate demand." https://a16z.com/the-marketplace-glossary/ (2020)
- **a16z, 13 Metrics for Marketplace Companies (Jordan, Jin, Coolican, Chen, 2020).** Match Rate: "How successfully can the two sides of the marketplace find each other?"; Market Depth: "Is there enough supply? Does it fit users' needs?"; Time to Match; Concentration/Fragmentation; Take Rate: "How valuable is the marketplace?"; Multi-Tenanting Prevalence; retention cohorts; Power User Curves. https://a16z.com/13-metrics-for-marketplace-companies/ (2020)
- **a16z Marketplace 100 (2023 edition, latest found).** "Now we're entering what we see as the third wave: fully curated marketplaces. These platforms are opinionated." "If we were to break it out as its own category, mental health would take the crown in terms of year-over-year GMV growth." https://a16z.com/marketplace-100/ (2023)
- **Lenny Rachitsky, "How to Kickstart and Scale a Marketplace" (2019).** Part 1 (Nov 20, 2019): "the best way to get big is by first going small"; nearly every successful marketplace constrained launch by geography or category (Etsy: vintage, craft supplies, handmade; TaskRabbit: handyman, cleaning, moving). https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace (2019). Part 2 (Nov 22, 2019): 80% (14 of 17) focused early on supply; DoorDash: "The largest growth lever has always been the supply"; Rover was the exception: "Supply was easy...Demand was much harder because we had to change customer behavior." https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-9ee (2019). Part 4 (Dec 3, 2019): initial demand came from word of mouth (over 50% of companies), supply driving demand (over 40%), SEO (over 40%). https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace-2e5 (2019)
- **Sequoia, "Writing a Business Plan."** "Start here: define your company in a single declarative sentence." Includes a "Why now?" slide: why hasn't this existed before, what market conditions enable it. https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/ (undated, current)
- **YC, Kevin Hale, "How to Pitch Your Startup."** Investors ask: "Is the idea understandable? Are they excited by it? Do they like the team and want to work with them?"; "a legible idea is understandable by people who know nothing about your business". https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6q-how-to-pitch-your-startup (2019), quotes via https://summify.io/discover/kevin-hale-how-to-pitch-your-startup-17XZGU/. His companion talk on evaluating ideas lists problem qualities (popular, growing, urgent, expensive, mandatory, frequent) **[paraphrase; verbatim not retrieved this session]** https://www.ycombinator.com/library/6h-how-to-evaluate-startup-ideas.

### 2c. Comparable markets and take rates (for the "cents per message, expert keeps 85%" line)

- Expert networks: "~$3Bn in 2025, growing ~12% annually (2023-25)"; GLG and AlphaSights largest. https://inex.one/blog/expert-network-market-size (updated Feb 9, 2026)
- JustAnswer (pay-per-question expert marketplace): "between 8,500 and 9,500 questions per day"; "over 12,000 experts"; "over 150 categories and 700 specialties"; customers pay "$28 and $125 per month, depending on the category"; "Experts keep between 20% and 50% of the fee". https://wallethacks.com/justanswer-review/ (updated Dec 23, 2023). So JustAnswer's take is 50-80%; the team's 15% take is radically more expert-friendly.
- Patreon platform fee: "10% of the income you earn on Patreon". https://www.patreon.com/pricing (2026)
- a16z glossary: take rate "usually varies from a low single-digit percent to the mid-30s" **[snippet]** https://a16z.com/the-marketplace-glossary/

### 2d. The questions a seed investor will ask this team (derived from the above)

1. Liquidity and match rate: when a hirer arrives, is there an expert agent that fits, and how fast? (a16z 13 metrics)
2. Who brings demand? Supply is "economically motivated" and easier; "the harder part... is figuring out how to aggregate demand" (a16z). Which side does the on-screen ICP prove?
3. Frequency: how often does this hirer come back? (Gurley) A resume review is once per job hunt; a pet is recurring; a lease is once a year.
4. Take rate vs. leakage: at 15% take with the human one click away, why won't the hirer and the expert transact off-platform? (a16z leakage; Gurley payment flow)
5. Fragmentation: are both sides fragmented enough that no incumbent aggregates them? (Gurley)
6. Wedge and constraint: which single category and campus do you launch in? (Lenny: "first going small")
7. Why now: what changed in 2025-2026 that makes this possible and necessary? (Sequoia)
8. Trust: how does the agent avoid hallucinating in the expert's name? (Dewey; Delphi leniency setting)
9. Founder-market fit: why this team, why this domain? (NFX; Rice "coachable")
10. Differentiation: vs. Delphi (creators with audiences), JustAnswer (anonymous experts, high take), free LLMs.

- Undergrads can show fit by selling to fellow students where they have "a deep understanding and unique insights of their customer base" **[snippet; attributed to startups.com/bizplan "Founder-Market Fit"; page not fetched]** https://www.bizplan.com/library/expert-advice/founder-market-fit

How teams frame "we are the customer" credibly (synthesized from the above and the winners in section 3):
1. Show your own receipt first: "I sent 80 applications since August; here is the spreadsheet." A number and an artifact beat an adjective.
2. Then show that you are not the only one: N fellow students validated this weekend, with one verbatim quote and the count on screen (the DoIT challenge's own ask).
3. Name the expert and the interview: "We interviewed [name], a recruiter at [company], for 40 minutes on Saturday." That is the supply-side founder story.
4. Show the refusal on camera. Admitting what the agent does not know is the fastest way to signal coachability and honesty, which Rice and NFX both prize.
5. Say what you learned that a non-student would not know (the Handshake application counts, the exact fair dates, what SuccessWorks does and does not do). That is the "unique insight" PG and Hale both ask for.
6. Avoid "everyone." One user, one moment, one document.

## 6. Scoring rubric, scores, and ranking

### 6a. Rubric

Scores are 1-5 per dimension. Weights sum to 100. Expert reachability is also a gate: if the team cannot interview and publish a real expert by Sunday morning, the scenario is dead regardless of score.

| # | Dimension | Weight | Tied to judging language |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Problem recognizability to this judge pool (VCs, TEL/WSB faculty, Google/Databricks engineers, DoIT staff, vet school) | 15 | "Does this project address a real-world problem" (BuildFest); "Is there a real need?" (Rice); "clearly defined, real friction point" (DoIT); Hale's "Is the idea understandable?" |
| B | Concreteness of the single user and single moment (one person, one document, one deadline) | 10 | "How clearly did the team communicate their problem statement" (BuildFest); "Ambiguity is a red flag" (Devpost judge); Sequoia "single declarative sentence" |
| C | Demo-ability in 60 seconds: upload, cited answer in the expert's words, refusal + link to human, with a document safe to show on camera | 15 | "Presentation & Demo" (BuildFest); "The demo video gives the most amount of scope" (Devpost); Cal Hacks winner: demo is "the most important part" |
| D | Receipts feasibility on campus this weekend (can you get 15-30 fellow students to validate by Sunday morning?) | 12 | "evidence you validated with fellow students this weekend" (DoIT); "tested assumptions" (Bradley 2025); Transcend winners' "100 accounts" |
| E | Expert reachability this weekend (a practicing expert with no audience who will sit for an interview and be published) | 10 | Supply-first (Lenny: 80% of marketplaces); "Market Depth: Is there enough supply?" (a16z); this is the product, not a nicety |
| F | Founder-market fit for a student team ("we are the customer," credibly) | 8 | "Build what you know, and for who you know" (Devpost); NFX Obsession/Founder Story; Strato's "founder-market fit" |
| G | Liability / regulatory cleanliness (does the refusal read as a feature, or does the category read as unlicensed advice?) | 8 | FTC v. DoNotPay (2025); a16z "managed marketplace... high-stakes categories"; Dewey on graceful failure |
| H | Implied audience size and scalability (TAM in one line; Gurley frequency) | 8 | "potential to grow into a viable product, venture" (BuildFest); "Is the problem big enough to build a company around?" (Rice); Gurley factors 6 and 8 |
| I | Investor "why now" strength (evidence that 2025-2026 changed the problem) | 7 | Sequoia "Why now?" slide; Strato "market signal" |
| J | Fit across challenges (Badgers, Open Venture, Art of the Break, Cost-of-Care) | 7 | Each challenge is $500-$1,000; a scenario that scores in three is worth more than one that scores in one |

Why these weights: A, C and D carry 42 points because the BuildFest criteria "Impact & Feasibility" and "Presentation & Demo" are half the official rubric and the DoIT challenge explicitly demands weekend validation. H and I are lower than a VC would weight them because Sunday's judges score a 2-minute video first and an investment memo second; the investor-heavy sensitivity run below shows the ranking barely moves.
