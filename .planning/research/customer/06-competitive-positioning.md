## 0. The position in one paragraph

Every alternative a hirer has today forces a trade: **generic-but-instant-and-free** (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Reddit) or **accountable-but-slow-and-expensive** (a CPA at $129–250/hr, a $349 college-counseling hour, a $99 fifteen-minute telehealth visit, a $65/month JustAnswer membership). The AI-clone tools that could bridge that gap (Delphi, Coachvox, BuddyPro) are priced and positioned for people who already have an audience, and OpenAI's GPT Store, the one mass-market attempt at a creator marketplace, retires on 11 Dec 2026 with its revenue program never having left a US pilot. The open slot is **named-practitioner accountability at chatbot price and speed, where the platform, not the expert, supplies the demand.** That is the shelf we sit on, and the rest of this document is the evidence.

## 4. Differentiators, ranked by defensibility

**1. Experts-without-audience discovery (most defensible).** This is a business-model choice, not a feature, and it is the one competitors are economically committed *against*. Delphi's tier names ("growing audiences", "celebrities and public figures", "White-Glove Set Up") and Coachvox's "connect your Stripe account, set your price" flow both assume the creator drives traffic. Intro.co charges 30% precisely when *it* brings the booking and 10% when you do — its pricing tells you demand is the scarce thing. Who could copy it: **JustAnswer** is the real threat; it already has category demand and credentialed experts. Speed: 6–12 months, but it cannibalizes their $65/mo subscription while their brand is under an FTC complaint. **Delphi** could open Discover as a true marketplace in 3–6 months, but would have to reprice from SaaS-plus-15% to usage. Defensibility is a demand-side habit: being the first place a hirer goes to ask a named tax preparer a question.

**2. Interview-first tacit-knowledge capture.** Every clone tool is upload-first: Delphi meters "training words", Coachvox and BuddyPro ingest existing content. That excludes exactly our supply side, practitioners with no content library. An LLM interviewer that turns answers into retrievable chunks has academic precedent ([arXiv 2603.14541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14541); [arXiv 2508.19942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19942)). Who could copy: any clone platform or OpenAI, in 1–3 months, because the interviewer is a prompt. What they can't copy quickly is the data exhaust: which questions, per category, produce chunks that get retrieved and rated well. Treat the interview as a learning system (question bank tuned by retrieval hit-rate), not a feature, or it is a six-week head start.

**3. Citation-grounded refusal.** RAG grounding is table stakes; Delphi already markets it. The refusal is the differentiator, and it is defensible only because it is *unpopular*: engagement-priced platforms (Poe per-message, Delphi/Coachvox subscriptions) lose money when the bot says "my expert hasn't covered that, contact them." The evidence that refusal is the right call is strong: models "hallucinate at least 75% of the time" on a court's core ruling (Stanford), and citation generation hallucinates 14–95% (GhostCite). Copy speed: weeks, technically. Incentive to copy: low for anyone paid by engagement. Our cost-based credits make refusal cheap for us.

**4. Transparent cost-based credits (1 credit = 1 cent, raw LLM cost × expert multiplier).** Poe's documented intent is to "cover all model inference costs" inside a points subscription; OpenAI's builder program pays "based on usage" via an undisclosed formula. Transparency is trivially copyable in code and hard to copy in practice because incumbents' margins live in the opacity. Copy speed: weeks; likelihood: low. Note this is a *supply-side* differentiator (experts can reason about earnings) more than a demand-side one.

**5. Self-only clones (least defensible).** A policy line: you may only clone yourself. It matters for trust (no unauthorized "AI Dave Ramsey") but any competitor can adopt it in a day. Keep it as a trust signal on the credibility card, not a pitch line.

## 5. The three sharpest answers

### "Why not just use ChatGPT?"

1. **On the exact tasks our seed categories cover, frontier models are measurably wrong and don't know it.** State-of-the-art models "succeed in calculating less than a third of federal income tax returns even on this simplified sample set," because they "misuse tax tables, make errors in tax calculation, and incorrectly determine eligibility" ([TaxCalcBench, Jul 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16126)). On legal questions, hallucination runs 69–88% with "a tendency towards overconfidence, irrespective of their actual accuracy" ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/hallucinating-law-legal-mistakes-large-language-models-are-pervasive)). Our agent answers only from the named expert's chunks, shows the chunk, and refuses otherwise.

2. **The problem isn't the model's knowledge, it's the human-model interaction.** In a 1,298-participant Oxford study, LLMs alone identified the right condition 94.9% of the time, but people *using* those same LLMs got it right under 34.5%, "no better than the control group" ([Bean et al., Apr 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18919)). A generic assistant waits for you to ask the right question. An interview-built agent carries the practitioner's questions ("did you get a 1099-K?", "where exactly does it hurt on stairs?") because the interview captured how the expert *works*, not just what they know.

3. **People already pay for a name, and they already distrust the nameless answer.** Only 29% of adults trust AI chatbots for reliable health information, and under half are confident they can tell true from false ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-misinformation-and-trust/poll-finding/kff-health-misinformation-tracking-poll-artificial-intelligence-and-health-information/)). Reddit's S-1 says people "add 'reddit' by name to their searches for virtually anything" because they want a human behind the answer ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1713445/000162828024006294/reddits-1q423.htm)). ChatGPT cannot tell you *who* is answering. We show a licensed PT's name, credentials, and the exact paragraph she wrote, with a "contact the expert" off-ramp.

### "Why not just hire the human?"

1. **The human is priced for the hour, and most questions are two minutes long.** A basic 1040 with state costs $317, itemized $376, and advice runs $129–250/hr (NATP 2025 via [Ramsey](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/taxes/how-much-does-a-tax-pro-cost)); a one-hour college strategy session is $349 ([Princeton Review](https://www.princetonreview.com/college-admissions/college-counseling)); fifteen telehealth minutes are $99 ([Doctor On Demand](https://doctorondemand.com/how-it-works/)); JustAnswer wants $65/mo behind a $5 "join" fee the FTC calls deceptive ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/01/ftc-sues-justanswer-deceiving-consumers-enrolling-costly-recurring-monthly-subscription)). At raw cost × 1–5x, a message costs cents. This is not a substitute for the $376 return; it is the answer to the fifty questions you'd never book an hour for.

2. **The human is not available when the question happens.** TurboTax Live experts work 5 AM–9 PM PT from 5 Jan to 20 Apr ([TurboTax](https://turbotax.intuit.com/personal-taxes/online/live/)); JustAnswer took 36 minutes in a published test ([Clark](https://clark.com/education/justanswer-review/)); mental-health telehealth is "within 1–2 days" ([Doctor On Demand](https://doctorondemand.com/how-it-works/)); Intro sessions are booked in 15-minute blocks at $35–$500 ([intro.co/experts](https://intro.co/experts)). The agent is the expert's method at 11 PM in September, and it hands you to the human when it hits the edge of what she's told it.

3. **Hiring the human is what the agent is for.** The agent is the cheapest possible way to find out whether *this* practitioner thinks the way you need before you pay for her hour, and for the expert it is a lead-qualified funnel that answers the repetitive 80% so her paid hours go to the hard 20%. Delphi's flagship case shows the ceiling: one expert, 2.5M+ conversations ([Delphi](https://www.delphi.ai/blog)). No human calendar does that; no human needs to.

## 6. Category name candidates (what shelf we sit on)

| Candidate | Shelf it implies | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expert agent marketplace** | Marketplaces (Upwork, JustAnswer) | Says two-sided, says agents, says discovery | "Agent" is crowded and vague in 2026 |
| **Ask-an-expert, AI-answered** | JustAnswer's shelf | Hirers already search "ask a tax expert"; frames us against the incumbent that is under FTC fire | Sounds like a feature, not a company |
| **Digital mind / knowledge-clone marketplace** | Delphi's shelf | Borrows an existing mental model | Delphi owns "digital mind"; the shelf signals creators-with-audiences, which is the opposite of our wedge |
| **Practitioner-grounded AI** | Vertical AI / trust | Names the differentiator (grounded, named practitioner) | Doesn't say marketplace |
| **Micro-consulting marketplace** | Clarity/Intro shelf | Says cheap, granular, expert | Implies human time |
| **Expert-in-the-loop advice** | HITL AI | Accurate | Jargon for hirers |

**Recommendation:** sit on the **ask-an-expert shelf** (JustAnswer's), not the creator-tool shelf (Delphi's). Demand-side users search by problem and category, not by "digital mind"; the incumbent on that shelf charges $65/mo and is being sued for how it bills; and the shelf's core promise (a credentialed human's answer) is exactly what our citations and credibility card deliver. Working category name: **"expert-grounded AI advice marketplace"**; the short pitch-line form: **"Ask a real expert's AI. Pay by the message."**
